Amlin vs Otter.ai

These are two different kinds of tool. Otter sends OtterPilot into your meeting as a participant and keeps the recording in the cloud. Amlin is a notepad on your own Mac: no bot in the call, no audio stored, and dictation and to-dos in the same app.

Facts checked August 2026 against Otter's public pricing and documentation. Spot something outdated? Tell us.

The short answer

Otter is a cloud transcription service you reach through the web, mobile apps, and a bot that joins your calls. Plans meter transcription by the minute, with a per-conversation cap on every tier.

Amlin is a native Mac app that captures the call from your own machine, never stores audio, never cuts a meeting off mid-conversation, and includes system-wide dictation and a cross-meeting to-do list.

On price: monthly billing favors Amlin ($10 vs $16.99). Annual billing favors Otter Pro slightly (about $8.33 a month), and we'd rather show you that than round it away.

Which one fits you

Choose Amlin if…

  • You don't want clients or teammates greeted by a bot named OtterPilot in the participant list.
  • "We never store your audio" matters to you; Amlin keeps text only, and dictation runs on-device.
  • You've been burned by a 30- or 90-minute cap cutting a real meeting in half.
  • You want dictation and a cross-meeting to-do list from the same $10 app.

Choose Otter if…

  • You need to work from Windows, Android, or the browser; Amlin is Mac-only.
  • You want playback of the original meeting recording, which requires storing it.
  • Your team shares a transcription workspace with admin controls.
  • You'll commit to annual billing and want the lowest sticker price (about $8.33 a month).

Questions, answered

Is Amlin a good Otter.ai alternative?

If you work on a Mac and don't want a bot in your meetings, yes. Amlin captures system audio from your own machine, so nothing joins the call, audio is never stored, and meetings are never cut off by a per-conversation cap. Otter is the better fit if you need Windows, Android, or web access, or your team lives in Otter's shared workspace.

Does Amlin join meetings the way OtterPilot does?

No, and this is the deepest difference between the two. OtterPilot joins your meeting as a visible participant that other attendees see and sometimes have to admit. Amlin captures your Mac's audio at the operating-system level: the participant list shows only people, and nobody else installs or approves anything.

Does Otter or Amlin store meeting recordings?

Otter keeps audio recordings in your account so you can play them back; that's a real feature, and it requires storing the audio. Amlin makes the opposite trade: sound is transcribed live and discarded on the spot, so there's no recording to leak, subpoena, or worry about. Only your notes and the transcript text remain. The full picture is in our privacy policy.

How do the free plans compare?

Otter's free plan includes 300 transcription minutes a month, but each conversation caps at 30 minutes, so a normal meeting gets cut off mid-sentence. Amlin's free plan includes 4 meeting-hours a month, a meeting in progress always finishes, your full note history is kept, and dictation is included and never metered.
